# Portfolio content system

## Case-study heroes

Each hero should make the project legible to a hiring manager in one scan.

### Metadata line

Use `Year · Company · Industry`. Keep industry names stable across projects.

### Title

- Aim for 45–60 characters and no more than two lines at the standard desktop width.
- Lead with the transformation or design action; add a verified scale signal where it strengthens the story.
- Use sentence case and an active construction.
- Avoid generic claims such as “innovative”, “seamless” or “world-class”.

### Tagline

- Use one sentence, ideally 12–20 words and approximately 100–130 characters for visual balance.
- Lead with the strongest verified outcome, then name the design intervention or mechanism.
- Distinguish measured results from projections, prototypes and future phases.
- Qualify causality accurately: use “helped”, “contributed” or “with the potential to” when other initiatives or unlaunched work shaped the result.
- Preserve domain language that helps recruiters understand the problem space, such as “intranet”, “digital workplace”, “IT support” and “client-facing”.

### Multi-phase projects

When a project contains separate phases, keep their evidence separate in the tagline. For Dai Pai Dong, local SEO owns the Google Business Profile result; the later digital reservation and walk-in tool is a low-risk replacement for the legacy pen-and-paper process. Do not imply the operational tool produced the SEO result or that an unlaunched prototype delivered measured impact.

## Labels and badges

- Use three badges at most, ordered as: problem space, design capability, platform or medium.
- Keep role labels outcome-neutral and specific to the work performed.
- Use “Concept” when the work was not launched; use “Pilot” only after a pilot ran, not when sites were merely approved.
- Reserve “Impact” for measured outcomes. Label forecast figures as “Projected impact” or “Opportunity”.

## Snapshot structure

Use the same scan order on every case study:

1. `Problem`
2. `Intervention`
3. `Measured impact` or `Projected impact`
4. `Delivery status`
5. Project details: `Company`, `Industry`, `Role`, `Timeline`, `Scale`, `Team`

Keep shipped, measured work separate from prototypes, approvals and roadmap items. Delivery status should make the boundary explicit without forcing a recruiter to infer it from the narrative.
